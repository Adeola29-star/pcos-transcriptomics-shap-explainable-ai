# One-Page Conclusion: Is the Model's Reasoning Biologically Credible?

This project trained a logistic regression classifier to distinguish PCOS from control samples using bulk RNA-seq expression data (GSE138518, 3 PCOS vs. 3 control granulosa cell samples), then applied SHAP to interpret its predictions. The central question is not whether the model achieved high accuracy (it did not) but whether its internal reasoning, as revealed by SHAP, reflects genuine, defensible biology or statistical artifact.

**The model does not generalize.** Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation, using a feature-selection pipeline built specifically to avoid data leakage, produced 0/6 correct predictions. Confidence scores confirmed this was not simple uncertainty-driven guessing: several predictions (e.g., 82-90% confidence) were wrong with high confidence. With only 5 training samples per fold against 33,289 candidate genes, this is the expected behavior of a severely underdetermined statistical problem, not evidence that PCOS lacks a real transcriptional signature.

**SHAP revealed a mix of plausible signal and likely noise.** Of the 30 genes selected by the final model (fit on all 6 samples), the majority were uncharacterized long non-coding RNAs or ESTs with no established function which is consistent with overfitting to this small sample. However, three top-ranked genes **FPR2, VNN3, and CCL25** have genuine, literature-supported relevance to PCOS. All three relate to inflammation or oxidative stress, both well-documented PCOS mechanisms, and SHAP's direction (verified against the model's raw decision function) correctly showed higher expression of each pushing toward a PCOS prediction.

**An important caveat about what SHAP explanations actually represent.** Examining an individual sample (P16, a true PCOS case) showed the final model explaining its prediction for that sample confidently and correctly. However, this same sample was one of the LOOCV-evaluated model's most confidently *wrong* predictions when held out during training. This contrast demonstrates that a SHAP explanation of a model fit on all available data reflects how that model rationalizes data it has already seen is not a validated, generalizable biological claim. Explainability and validity are different properties, and this project's results make the distinction concrete.

**The STRING and NetworkAnalyst cross-check added a further honest nuance.** Using two independent interaction databases, none of the 8 named, literature-recognized genes from the SHAP analysis showed any documented or predicted protein-protein interactions with each other. This means the model's top genes, while individually biologically plausible, do not form a validated, coordinated pathway though they may reflect genuinely independent biological signals, or simply several separate coincidental associations in a very small dataset.

**Overall judgment:** the model's reasoning shows a genuine mix of credible and non-credible signal. A small number of its most influential genes are biologically sensible and directionally consistent with established PCOS pathophysiology, which is a meaningful finding on its own. But the model as a whole does not generalize, most of its selected features are uncharacterized and more consistent with noise than biology, and none of its biologically plausible genes form a validated interacting network. This project's value lies not in having built a working diagnostic classifier (it has not) but in demonstrating the disciplined, honest process required to distinguish a model's genuine biological insight from its statistical artifacts, which is the actual purpose of applying explainable AI rather than treating a model as a black box.
